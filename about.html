About the taskforce

A Stool mandate to keep Adabraka clean.

Sanitation Soldiers began with a decision by the Adabraka Atukpai Mantse: the sanitation of Adabraka would no longer wait on someone else.

Why we exist

The problem was never a secret.

Adabraka grew fast and built up. Compounds that once held one family now hold many tenants. Waste volumes rose, drains narrowed under silt and plastic, and each rainy season brought the same flood to the same streets.

Residents knew the problems and reported them. What the community did not have was a standing team to receive those reports and act on them. The Stool Authority created Sanitation Soldiers to be that team.

See what we do

Adabraka, in brief

Adabraka is a historic suburb of Accra, laid out in the early 1900s and today part of the Korle Klottey Municipal Assembly. It sits close to the central business district, between Ring Road and the Kwame Nkrumah Interchange.

Its density is the source of its life and the source of its sanitation pressure. The taskforce works inside that reality, not around it.

Who we are

A community taskforce under the Stool.

Sanitation Soldiers is made up of local recruits and coordinators drawn from Adabraka, working under the authority of the Adabraka Atukpai Stool Authority.

Under the Stool Authority

The Adabraka Atukpai Mantse and the Office of the Adabraka Atukpai Stool Authority founded the taskforce and hold it to account. That backing carries the trust of the traditional authority into day-to-day sanitation work.

Alongside the Assembly

We are not the Assembly and we do not replace it. The Korle Klottey Municipal Assembly makes and enforces sanitation by-laws and runs waste collection. We support that work with a daily presence the Assembly cannot always keep on every street.

Our mandate

What the Stool charged us to do.

Every action the taskforce takes traces back to the mandate set by the Stool Authority. The mandate is the line we answer to, and the promise the community holds us to.

  • Clear and desilt the drains, culverts and channels that block and flood.
  • Watch the public toilets, waste points and markets, and report failures.
  • Help residents and businesses store, separate and hand over waste correctly.
  • Document sanitation offences and back the Assembly's enforcement.
  • Run education and clean-up days that build lasting habits.
  • Receive and answer reports from the community.

How a soldier of the taskforce carries themselves

Authority on a street is earned by conduct. These are the standards every member of the taskforce works to.

  • We identify ourselves by name and show the authority of the Stool.
  • We record a site before we touch it.
  • We follow the Assembly's by-laws in every action we take.
  • We treat every resident with respect, including those we correct.
  • We reply to every report, even when the fix belongs to another office.
Our limits

What we will not do.

Credibility comes from being clear about the edges of our role. The community should know exactly what the taskforce is not.

  • We do not collect money or fees of any kind.
  • We do not enter private property without consent or lawful authority.
  • We do not punish or fine anyone. Enforcement and sanctions belong to the Assembly.
  • We do not promise a fix that is not ours to make. We will tell you where it belongs.

If anyone claims to act for Sanitation Soldiers and asks you for money, refuse and report it to the Stool Authority office.

Governance

Accountable to the Stool, co-ordinated with the Assembly.

The taskforce reports to the Office of the Adabraka Atukpai Stool Authority, which set it up and holds it to account. It co-ordinates its work with the Korle Klottey Municipal Assembly, the constituency's representatives and community groups.

Records of the reports we receive and the actions we take stay with the Stool Authority office. Where a problem sits with another office or is above what the taskforce can fix, we say so and follow it up.

Work with the taskforce.

Invite us to a clean-up, a school programme or a community meeting, or send a report about a problem near you.
